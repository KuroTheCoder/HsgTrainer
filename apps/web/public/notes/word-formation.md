# Word formation strategies

Word formation questions give you a base word and ask for a derived form. Most misses aren't about vocabulary — they're about **not checking the part of speech the gap needs**.

## The 4-question checklist

Run this on every gap:

1. What part of speech does the sentence require? (noun, verb, adjective, adverb)
2. Does the base word need a prefix, a suffix, or both?
3. Is the answer **countable vs uncountable**, or a **person vs an abstract noun**?
4. Does the surrounding grammar force a special form (plural, negative, comparative)?

```text
base: "rely" → gap needs a noun describing a person → "reliant"? No.
"reliant" is an adjective. The noun is "reliance", the person is "reliant" only
as an adjective — for a person noun use "rely-er"? No such word. It is "reliant"
as in "be reliant on"; the noun person form is "reliance" in "put reliance in".
Check a dictionary when unsure — keys routinely accept multiple forms.
```

## The common trap suffixes

| Needed part of speech | Typical endings | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Noun (abstract) | -tion, -ment, -ness, -ity | contribute → **contribution** |
| Noun (person) | -er, -or, -ist, -ian | economy → **economist** |
| Adjective | -ful, -less, -ive, -ous | hope → **hopeful** |
| Adverb | -ly | quick → **quickly** |
| Negative prefixes | un-, in-, im-, dis- | logical → **illogical** |

> [!warning] The classic miss
> Answers are normalized case- and space-insensitively, and trailing punctuation is stripped — but the key must still **match the exact form**. "Contribution" is not "contributing".

> [!example] Accepted variants
> HSG keys routinely accept multiple spellings or forms. The app's accepted variants list covers them — a wrong-looking answer that matches a variant still scores correct. See [[index]] for how the ledger clears old mistakes when you get a question right.
